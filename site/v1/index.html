How it worked

One loop, thirteen times a day

v1 traded ~670 liquid US stocks on 30-minute bars. Each completed bar was a decision point — thirteen per session — and every one of them ran the same five steps.

  1. Fill what was decided last barOrders queued on the previous bar executed at this bar's open, never at the price that triggered them. Fills paid a half-spread, slippage, square-root price impact, and regulatory fees. That one-bar delay is what made the backtest honest: a decision could never be filled at a price it had already seen.
  2. Mark the bookValue every holding at the bar's close, write a ledger row, and mark the buy-and-hold benchmarks alongside it.
  3. LearnEach stock's realized return over the bar became one online update to the model's weights. The policy was never frozen — it adapted continuously, every bar, all day.
  4. Score and decideRank all ~670 names by conviction — the model's appetite for owning each one. Sell anything whose conviction collapsed or that broke a volatility-scaled stop. Buy the strongest names, sized in proportion to conviction, at most 20% in any one, and scaled so the whole book sat at 90% of the portfolio. No cap on how many names it held — breadth made positions thinner, never borrowed.
  5. Go flat before the closeOn the 15:00 ET bar the entire book was sold to zero, and nothing new was opened that couldn't be closed the same day. v1 ended every session 100% cash, so it carried no overnight or weekend gap risk at all.
Under the hood

The parts worth naming

  • The policy was a single recurrent unit, not a deep network — Moody–Saffell direct reinforcement learning, f = tanh(w·x + u·f_prev + b) over 19 trailing-only price and volume features. It was trained to maximise a differential Sharpe ratio with trading costs inside the gradient, so the cost of a trade was part of what it learned, not a penalty applied afterwards.
  • It read the news. Every weekend a separate job collected free economy and company news, scored each story with a financial-specific sentiment lexicon, and published a per-stock score. During the week that score nudged conviction multiplicatively — it could resize and reorder the book, but it could never open a position the price model was neutral on.
  • No kill switch, deliberately. A latching halt assumes a book meant to persist and an operator on hand to restart it; v1 was flat every close and unattended. An earlier replay showed exactly the failure mode: a model-health switch fired two hours in and the book sat in cash for five weeks, including through the recovery. Risk was carried continuously instead — per-name volatility-scaled stops, a re-entry cooldown, de-grossing after stop-outs, and a flat book at every close.
  • Look-ahead was impossible by construction, and tested for: features are trailing-only, and a test corrupts future bars then asserts the past is bit-identical. A separate test requires that churning a driftless random walk with costs loses money — no free money on noise.
Why it was retired

It worked exactly as designed, and that was the problem

v1's plumbing was never the issue. Across 19 sessions the cron never missed a bar, the book stayed within its exposure cap, the model kept learning, and it went flat before every close as instructed. It simply had no edge to trade.

A coin flip, paying costs, 13.8× over
v1 closed 2,508 round-trips at a 50.0% win rate. That is not a losing strategy — it is no strategy, a fair coin. Meanwhile it turned the entire book over 13.8× in 19 sessions. A coin flip is free; a coin flip that pays a spread on every flip bleeds. Final result: −3.79% against +5.47% for simply holding its own universe.

None of this was a surprise, which is the important part. The research log had already measured it twice: cross-sectional price-and-volume features on liquid US megacaps produced a rank information coefficient of +0.004 to +0.007 against a +0.02 go/no-go bar — a miss of roughly 3×. Worse, the 20-day version of the signal turned out to be a 2020–2021 artifact, flat to negative in every year since. More feature engineering was not going to fix it.

But the same research found where the edge actually was. Sweeping the prediction horizon turned up a 3-day signal that was statistically significant (t = 2.64) and regime-persistent — positive in 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026, not just the meme years. Traded as a dollar-neutral long/short book it was net-positive out-of-sample through roughly 3 bp per side:

v1 structurally could not trade it
The edge is dollar-neutral, and v1 was long-only — a long-only book cannot be neutral. The edge is a 3-day signal, and v1 liquidated to cash every afternoon — it could not hold a position long enough for the signal to pay. The edge is cost-limited at ~3 bp/side, and v1 round-tripped its entire book daily. Every property that made v1 safe also made this particular edge unreachable. That is why it was retired rather than tuned: the successor needed to be able to hold a short overnight, and no amount of parameter changes gets a long-only day-trader there.
The successor

What v2 changes

v1 — retiredv2 — live
EngineBespoke Python bar loopNautilusTrader — Rust-native, event-driven; the same engine and execution semantics run the backtest and the live loop
DirectionLong onlyLong and short — the book can be market-neutral
Holding periodFlat by every closeHeld overnight, so a multi-day signal can actually pay out
Exposure≤ 90% of cash, never borrowedMargin account, leverage permitted
SignalRecurrent RRL conviction, long-only rankingCross-sectional short-horizon ranker, de-meaned so it is a relative bet rather than a market bet
NewsMultiplicative nudge on conviction; could not originate a positionAdditive on the ranked score, so news can originate a short; macro tone tilts net exposure
Kill switchNone, deliberatelyNone, deliberately — same argument, same continuous risk controls
Capital$100,000 simulated$100,000 simulated, reset to cash at v2's inception

v2 is unproven. The strategy it is built to express was profitable in an out-of-sample backtest at a Sharpe of about 0.2 — thin enough that execution cost decides it, and thin enough that a few weeks of forward results will not settle anything either. v1's honest epitaph is that it ran flawlessly and lost 3.8%; v2 starts with no more right to be believed than that. The forward record is the only test.
